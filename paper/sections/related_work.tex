\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Retention in continual RL.}
Methods that preserve earlier tasks fall into three families
\citep{pan2025survey}. \emph{Regularisation} penalises movement in parameters
estimated to matter for past tasks, weighted by Fisher information
\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc} or applied through distillation into a consolidated
network \citep{schwarz2018progress}, which requires fixing a penalty weight
before it is known which tasks will come under pressure. \emph{Rehearsal}
stores past experience and replays it, as in CLEAR \citep{rolnick2019clear},
which clones behaviour and value towards replayed trajectories; the buffer is a
proxy for the objective, since reproducing past behaviour is not the same as
still achieving past return, and storage grows with the sequence.
\emph{Architecture-based} methods allocate or freeze parameters per task, as
new columns \citep{rusu2016progressive}, pruned subnetworks
\citep{mallya2018packnet}, self-composing modules \citep{malagon2024componet},
or an adapted pool of task-specific knowledge vectors \citep{hu2025ckarl},
where frozen capacity cannot be overwritten but also cannot improve, so
positive backward transfer is unavailable by construction. Meta-learning
approaches \citep{finn2017maml} and further variants within each family are
surveyed by \citet{pan2025survey}.

\paragraph{Loss of plasticity.}
A distinct failure is the loss of the ability to learn anything new after
prolonged training, which \citet{abbas2023plasticity} mitigate by replacing the
activation with a concatenated ReLU and \citet{dohare2024plasticity} by
continually reinitialising low-utility units. These restore the capacity to
acquire rather than the capacity to hold, and the two failures are separable.
In Section~\ref{sec:results} both improve forward transfer while leaving
backward transfer indistinguishable from naive fine-tuning. We make no
plasticity claim, and neither intervention conflicts with the objective
proposed here.

\paragraph{Constrained RL.}
Constrained Markov decision processes \citep{altman1999cmdp} pose policy
optimisation subject to explicit constraints, solved either by
feasibility-preserving trust regions \citep{achiam2017cpo} or by Lagrangian
relaxation with dual ascent on the multiplier \citep{tessler2019rcpo}. There
the constraint is an exogenous budget on an auxiliary cost, fixed in advance
and independent of learning. We apply the same machinery against a comparator
that is learned and that moves, namely a specialist trained on each task as it
arrives.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Across these families retention is approximated rather than required. It is
priced by a penalty weight, approximated by a stored sample, or bought with
frozen parameters. We state it directly, requiring that the deployed policy
stay within a tolerance of a task-specific specialist on every task it has
seen, and enforce that requirement by primal-dual alternation so each task's
multiplier is set by the optimisation rather than chosen in advance. The
constraint is one-sided, which separates it from both distillation and
freezing, since the deployed policy is prevented from falling behind a
specialist but left free to exceed it. Nothing is stored and nothing is frozen,
so retention does not compete with capacity as the sequence lengthens.
